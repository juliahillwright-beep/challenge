Retention: How I'll develop people

The cadence + rhythm that surfaces problems early.

Two stories

Promotion story: Noah Sakr

The gap: Noah had experience in Enterprise, but was unfamiliar with the pace + volume of Commercial.

What we did together: Weekly PG Plan reviews, mid-week strategy huddles, post-call debriefs to level up his skills.

Result: Noah became accustomed to the pace of Commercial business + confidence to step into next role as an AE.

Turnaround story: A familiar journey

Diagnosis: 112 at-bats with 7% win rate - assumptions we can make from the data are 1) No champ 2) Lack of pain 3) Progressing too fast through the cycle.

The Plan: Triage and get rep buy-in on where the breakdown is. Double down on pipe review to address gaps in pain + champ-building. Channel Danielle's famous words, "Add me to your calls".

Result, and what I learned: Spend more time qualifying earlier in the funnel to validate pain and champ to ensure we're spending time with the right accounts. Some (tenured) reps will have higher conversion and need less at-bats, others need more at-bats to learn before mastering the sale.  Getting in the boat together.

02

Retention: Culture & Motivation 

How I've impacted culture on my team

Culture in Action: Team JHW

As NewCo started to come together, I saw a gap in collaboration. Reps were afraid to swap notes on accounts, conflicting about when to pass leads, and LMS enablement was only taking us so far. To bridge the gap, I invited my BDR, SA, Fivetran counterpart, and their BDR to join a standing weekly meeting. Each week, I encouraged everyone to report on the state of the business from their corner as experts in their field. We'd identify trends, common struggles, opportunities to PG together, and ultimately--began building pipeline + closing deals together.

What I would launch in my first quarter as RDS

I began my career in sales development, and will tell anyone who will listen that SDRs + BDRs are the lifeblood of any GTM org, but rarely get the respect they deserve at the start of their careers. I believe BDRs should be regarded as "experts in their field" who can share outbound/PG best practices with AEs who can learn from them. I ran this series at GitLab and called it "Tips, Tricks & Tea" -- where AEs could come ask SDRs/BDRs what was working, what wasn't, and where to double down. We need this at NewCo-- to inspire AEs & to motivate BDRs.
